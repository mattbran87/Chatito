## Gains

- **Blockers fall from 9 to 6 (skill) and 5 (style).** A blocker is a false statement, an invented cause, a dropped warning, or a changed identifier.
- **Fact retention rises by 0.27 with the skill.** The hard floor ("a cut never removes a warning") does its job.
- **The rules do not cause invented causes.** `diagnose-partial` scores 4.75 with the skill against 4.22 for baseline, and Jev finds no invented cause in any condition.

## Losses

1. **Concision falls by 0.38 (skill) and 0.29 (style).** The rules make responses longer. This supports the planned "no preamble, no recap, no closers" rule.
2. **`toast` with the style scores 3.75, against 4.80 for baseline.** The style tells the model to "say plainly that the rules do not apply", so the toast opens with a remark about writing rules. The skill does not lose here, at 4.82.
3. **`commit-format` with the skill scores 4.28, against 4.70.** The prompt requires a `Resolves:` line, and the skill's word table bans "resolve". This supports the planned "the harness wins" rule.
4. **`explain-rebase` and `doc-readme-rewrite` score 0.2 lower with the skill.** No one read these responses for this write-up. Read them before cycle 2.

## Limits

- **Noise.** The overall weighted gaps are 0.08 to 0.10. The external harness reported a per-case standard deviation of up to 0.95, so 3 trials cannot separate these overall gaps from noise.
- **The linter is circular.** It checks the skill's own rules, so its row shows that the rules took effect, not that the prose is better.
- **One judge model.** The judge is the same model that wrote the responses. Jev is the cross-model check, and it is not calibrated yet.

## Cost

$8.60 of Claude calls recorded in the run folder, and about $0.01 of Jev calls. The first attempt, `2026-10-20-baseline`, recorded $2.23 and stopped on looping calls. The no-tools note fixed that, and no call in this run looped. The slowest calls took 22 s (generate) and 15 s (judge).

## Calibration

A person labeled 19 of the 20 sampled responses. No Jev question passed or failed, because the labels hold too few "yes" answers. A question needs at least 3 of each answer.

| `invents_cause` | 0 | 8 | 8 of 8 |
| `filler` | 0 | 19 | 12 of 19 |
| `idiom` | 0 | 19 | 17 of 19 |
| `drops_warning` | 0 | 7 | 7 of 7 |

- **`filler` is the least reliable question.** Jev scored 7 responses from 0.61 to 0.82 that the person marked clean. All 7 come from `diagnose-clear` and `diagnose-partial`. So the `filler` rates in the table above are too high.
- **The sample cannot calibrate Jev.** Almost every sampled response is clean, so there are no "yes" cases to agree on. The next sheet needs about 10 hand-written bad responses that hold real filler, idioms, and dropped warnings.
- All 5 questions stay report-only. The Claude judge and the linter decide cycle 2.
